Why Ketoacidosis is Rare in Type 2 Diabetes

An in-depth look at the mechanism of lipolysis inhibition and why residual insulin in Type 2 diabetics prevents the massive ketone production seen in DKA.
AdSense Horizontal Banner

It takes very little insulin to suppress Hormone Sensitive Lipase (HSL) in adipose tissue. Type 2 diabetics, despite having insulin resistance, usually produce enough endogenous insulin to block HSL.

Without active HSL, free fatty acids are not released in massive quantities, and the liver does not produce ketone bodies at a rate that overwhelms buffering capacity.
